\begin{abstract}
Digital Product Passports (DPPs) are emerging as a regulatory and industrial
mechanism for exposing technical, compliance, and sustainability information
about products, which auditors, regulators, and procurement teams must query
on demand. Reliable querying means answers grounded in source records,
auditable under scrutiny, and withheld when required information is missing
or uncertain. DPP evidence is heterogeneous, incomplete, and spread across
documents, datasheets, and ontologies; this limits fixed schema SQL or SPARQL
querying. Language models offer a flexible interface. However, retrieval
augmented and long context systems provide limited support for persistent
reuse of validated facts, machine checkable compliance reasoning, and
principled abstention when evidence is insufficient. We present \Compass, an
architecture that puts reliability first: every output is an evidence
grounded answer with provenance or an explicit abstention. It combines hybrid
retrieval, persistent fact memory, targeted symbolic validation over a DPP
ontology, and a selective decision layer with post hoc confidence
calibration. On a reliability suite of 3,429 DPP questions, \Compass\
improves accuracy over a strong retrieval baseline from 0.8973 to 0.9749 and
reduces the area under the risk--coverage curve (AURC) from 0.5513 to 0.0117.
On questions governed by rules, accuracy rises from 0.0102 to 0.7085; rules
fire on 7.96\% of the workload with an observed conditional precision of
1.000. On a verified release of 6,270 questions (42\% rule governed) and a
compositional benchmark of 3,000 questions, \Compass\ reaches the ceiling
under deterministic validators, while long context and neurosymbolic
baselines leave 127 to 321 errors. On 4,021 queries over the unseen Open Food
Facts schema, it achieves 0.972 decision accuracy at 0.730 answer coverage
with an expected calibration error of 0.021. Targeted symbolic control, persistent evidence reuse,
and selective abstention thus improve the reliability and auditability of
product record querying. Calibration on the source
domains remains an open challenge.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly used to query technical
documentation and semistructured product records. In regulated product
settings, the familiar failures of such models, including hallucinated
properties, unverified compliance claims, and overconfident answers, are not
merely usability issues: they propagate into audits, procurement decisions,
sustainability reporting, and downstream supply chain actions. Digital Product
Passports (DPPs) sharpen these requirements by moving product information
toward standardized, inspectable disclosure across the product life cycle,
where decisions must remain defensible under external scrutiny
\cite{eu2024espr,ec_espr_dpp}. The central concern is therefore not whether an
answer sounds plausible, but whether it can be verified, traced back to
evidence, and acted upon safely.

This work originates in an applied setting. \Compass\ was developed within
CE-RISE, an EU Horizon Europe project on circular economy resource
information systems, and exercised on real product documentation collected
through the project's industrial partners in the printing hardware and
heating system domains, alongside a public battery corpus.
An interactive DPP workbench built on \Compass, with a full audit panel
(retrieved memory and passages, fired rules, derived triples, confidence,
abstain status) plus carbon accounting and DPP validation, has been
demonstrated live to consortium practitioners outside the development team.

% if available; the In-Use track explicitly rewards it.
The target users are compliance, procurement, and sustainability
practitioners. For them, a confident but unsupported answer is more costly
than an explicit ``don't know.''

Querying DPP data must be reliable for three recurring reasons: answers
often depend on facts introduced much earlier and across multiple documents,
so evidence must be recalled over long horizons; many queries are governed
by rules (obligations, forbidden combinations, ageing constraints), so
correctness means satisfying structured conditions rather than producing
fluent text; and the system must abstain when evidence is missing, stale,
contradictory, or outside policy. Prompting alone guarantees none of this:
models with long context windows remain sensitive to where evidence appears
in the window \cite{liu2024lostinthemiddle}, and free text rationales may
not reflect the model's actual decision process
\cite{turpin2023unfaithfulcot,lanham2023faithfulnesscot}.

Most current systems for querying product documents rely on retrieval
augmented generation (RAG) or long context prompting for grounding. These
answer many factual questions but offer limited support for reusing verified
facts across sessions, for compliance decisions whose evidence a machine can
check, and for operating points that trade coverage against risk. We
therefore treat the choice between answering and abstaining as an explicit
decision, characterized with risk--coverage curves, AURC, and calibration
diagnostics so that operating points can be set by policy
\cite{geifman2019selectivenet,kamath2020selectiveqa,guo2017calibration,traub2024aurc}.

The foundation of \Compass\ is that decision, not any single knowledge
source: hybrid retrieval and persistent fact memory handle open book and
long horizon questions; a targeted symbolic layer validates compliance
queries against structured facts under a DPP ontology informed by the
CIRPASS-2 requirements specification and returns rule traces a machine can
check; and a calibration layer converts the evidence signals into answer or
abstain decisions tuned to a target coverage. The ontology and rules provide
auditable validity on the subset of queries where structured constraints
apply; they are not the sole knowledge store. The architecture transfers to
other structured product records through schema and ontology adaptation, not
a redesign of the decision interface.

\noindent Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=2pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{An architecture that puts reliability first} (\Compass),
  composing hybrid retrieval, persistent fact memory, and a targeted symbolic
  layer backed by a DPP ontology informed by the CIRPASS-2 requirements
  specification, behind an explicit
  interface that either answers with provenance or abstains.
  \item \textbf{An evaluation protocol for auditable, selective querying},
  pairing accuracy with Wilson confidence intervals \cite{wilson1927probable},
  selective risk (risk--coverage curves and AURC)
  \cite{kamath2020selectiveqa,traub2024aurc}, paired significance testing
  (McNemar) \cite{mcnemar1947note}, calibration diagnostics
  \cite{guo2017calibration}, and coverage and precision of the symbolic layer.
  \item \textbf{Three complementary empirical studies:} a reliability suite of
  3,429 questions on real partner and public corpora, scaled to a
  deterministically filtered, verified release of 6,270 questions (2,649
  governed by rules) for comparison against external baselines; a benchmark of 3,000 compositional questions
  built for this study, run against strong baselines with long context windows
  and neurosymbolic systems; and a generalization study of 4,021 queries from
  a different domain, Open Food Facts (drawn from a database of 4.5M
  products), which stresses calibration and abstention on an unseen schema.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related work}

\paragraph{Retrieval augmented generation and structured grounding.}
RAG improves factuality by conditioning the generator on retrieved passages
\cite{lewis2020rag}, and hybrid retrieval with fusion readers improves
evidence utilisation further \cite{izacard2021fid}. Retrieval alone,
however, enforces neither logical validity nor calibrated trust, and longer
context windows do not guarantee robust evidence use
\cite{liu2024lostinthemiddle}. A complementary tradition answers questions
over a knowledge graph as the primary store (KGQA), enforcing validity
through graph structure but presuming graph coverage that heterogeneous,
incomplete DPP corpora do not meet. \Compass\ occupies the ground between:
retrieval and memory are the primary evidence path, the symbolic layer is
invoked only on the compliance subset where structured validation adds
provable value, and the system degrades gracefully where the graph is
silent.

\paragraph{Memory augmented language models.}
External memory mechanisms range from differentiable controllers
\cite{graves2014ntm,graves2016dnc} to nonparametric retrieval in language
modelling \cite{khandelwal2020knnlm}; at scale, RETRO substitutes an
external corpus for parametric capacity \cite{borgeaud2022retro}, recurrent
memory transformers carry state beyond a single window \cite{bulatov2022rmt},
and agentic systems accumulate experience stores \cite{wang2023voyager}.
These lines optimise capacity or skill acquisition. Our requirement differs:
what must persist is not experience but \emph{verified facts}, and what
matters is auditability. \Compass\ therefore uses a deliberately narrow
memory of validated facts with provenance identifiers, retrieved by
similarity and merged into the evidence under the same citation discipline
as retrieved passages.

\paragraph{Neurosymbolic reasoning and verifiability.}
Neurosymbolic approaches combine learning with explicit inference to improve
verifiability and compositional generalisation
\cite{riegel2020lnn,dong2019nlm,manhaeve2018deepproblog}. In ontology
settings, OWL reasoners such as HermiT provide sound inference over
structured knowledge \cite{glimm2014hermit}, and the OWL~2 profiles define
fragments with favourable computational properties
\cite{motik2009owl2profiles}. In the LLM era, systems such as LINC and
Logic-LM couple language models to symbolic solvers to improve logical
reasoning \cite{olausson2023linc,pan2023logiclm}; our external comparisons use
prompted approximations of their procedures. What this literature rarely reports is where symbolic machinery
actually applies in a deployed workload and how reliable it is when it does.
We contribute exactly these measurements: symbolic \emph{coverage} (the
fraction of questions on which rules fire) and symbolic \emph{precision}
(correctness conditional on firing). This turns auditability from a design
claim into an empirically testable property and motivates the word
\emph{targeted}: rules fire on a small fraction of the workload with high
observed conditional precision (Section~\ref{sec:reliability}).

\paragraph{Calibration, selective prediction, and faithful reasoning.}
Neural networks are frequently miscalibrated
\cite{guo2017calibration,desai2020calibration}; selective prediction
formalises abstention under coverage constraints
\cite{geifman2019selectivenet,kamath2020selectiveqa}, with AURC as a
one-number summary \cite{traub2024aurc}. Natural language reasoning can be
effective yet unfaithful to the model's actual decision process
\cite{turpin2023unfaithfulcot,lanham2023faithfulnesscot}, and tool use in
the style of ReAct improves factuality without formal guarantees or
calibration \cite{yao2022react}. \Compass\ draws these threads together:
the answerer is constrained to sanctioned evidence, symbolic conclusions
carry traces independent of rationale fluency, and abstention is evaluated
with the selective prediction toolkit rather than asserted.

\paragraph{Positioning.}
Prior work provides the ingredients (retrieval, memory, symbolic inference,
calibration), but to our knowledge no product record querying system
composes them behind one answer or abstain interface whose components are
individually auditable and whose selective behaviour is measured end to end.
That composition and its evaluation protocol are this paper's contributions.

\section{Problem formulation and metrics}
Given a query $x$ about a specific product and its documentation, the system
outputs either (i) an answer $y$ with provenance (citations and/or a rule
trace) or (ii) an abstention symbol $\bot$. The system operates over a domain
corpus (technical documents) and a structured knowledge layer (ontology and
facts), and targets workflows where reliability is critical and ``don't
know'' is safer than a confident but unsupported answer. A confidence score
$c(x)\in[0,1]$ and a threshold $\tau$ define a selective predictor that
returns $y(x)$ when $c(x)\ge\tau$ and $\bot$ otherwise; \emph{coverage} is
the fraction of instances answered and \emph{risk} is the error rate among
answered instances. On datasets where some questions
are unanswerable by design, we additionally distinguish \emph{decision
accuracy}, which credits a correct answer or an abstention on an unanswerable
question, from \emph{answer accuracy}, correctness among answered questions
only, and we report abstention precision and recall against ground truth
answerability. We summarise the risk--coverage trade-off with AURC; lower
values indicate that errors concentrate at low confidence and can be avoided
by abstention \cite{kamath2020selectiveqa,traub2024aurc}. Traub et al.\ also
propose AUGRC as a refinement of AURC; we retain AURC for comparability with
prior selective QA work and release per instance predictions so that
alternative summaries can be recomputed. Operating points are policy
choices (a mandated minimum coverage in customer support, stricter abstention
in compliance auditing), so we report the entire curve rather than a single
threshold.

Calibration is reported as expected calibration error (ECE), the weighted
average gap between per-bin confidence and per-bin accuracy
\cite{guo2017calibration,desai2020calibration}. Because calibration is not
arithmetically implied by high accuracy, we treat it as a separate
reliability axis. To avoid over-interpreting small differences we report
Wilson score 95\% confidence intervals \cite{wilson1927probable}, which are
well behaved near 0 and 1, and McNemar's exact test for paired comparisons on
identical question sets \cite{mcnemar1947note}. Finally, we report symbolic
\emph{coverage} (fraction of questions triggering at least one rule) and
symbolic \emph{precision} (correctness conditional on firing), isolating
where explicit rules contribute and where the system relies on retrieval or
memory.

\section{The COMPASS architecture}

\subsection{Design principles and inference path}
Three principles shape the design. \emph{Evidence before generation}: the
answerer is instructed to compose only from evidence that the system has
explicitly gathered and sanctioned, and every claim carries a provenance
identifier from that evidence; this is a design discipline enforced through
prompting and audited through the surfaced citations, not a formal guarantee
that parametric knowledge cannot leak. \emph{Targeted
validity}: symbolic machinery is applied exactly where structured facts and
rules exist, so that its guarantees are real rather than diluted over
questions it cannot check. \emph{Selective output}: every response passes
through a calibrated answer or abstain decision, so the system's confidence
is a measured quantity with an operating point, not a stylistic property of
generated text.

Figure~\ref{fig:architecture} shows the resulting inference path. A query is
processed in four stages. (1)~\emph{Evidence acquisition}: hybrid retrieval
over the document corpus and similarity lookup in the persistent memory store
assemble candidate evidence. (2)~\emph{Symbolic validation}: if the query
touches structured facts, the ontology layer evaluates the applicable rules
and emits conclusions together with traces. (3)~\emph{Composition}: a context
bound LLM answerer drafts an answer using only the sanctioned evidence, with
every claim linked to a provenance identifier. (4)~\emph{Decision}: a
calibration layer maps the evidence signals to a confidence score, and the
threshold $\tau$ decides between an answer with provenance and a safe
abstention.

\subsection{Evidence acquisition: hybrid retrieval and persistent memory}
The retriever combines sparse lexical and dense embedding search with
optional reranking, consistent with evidence that hybrids outperform purely
dense or sparse retrieval on heterogeneous corpora
\cite{lewis2020rag,izacard2021fid}. Every returned passage carries a
provenance identifier. The prototype persistent memory stores timestamped
text entries within a session; at query time, entries are retrieved by
semantic similarity and merged with the retrieved passages. Memory serves questions
whose supporting facts entered the documentation long before the current
interaction, and it reduces dependence on fitting everything into one
context window.

The current memory store is scoped by session rather than by an independently
enforced product identifier. It does not yet enforce provenance validation,
immutable supersession, or correction history; these are requirements for a
production DPP deployment rather than properties evaluated in this study.

A recurring failure of document grounded QA is that the generator mixes
retrieved evidence with unsupported extrapolation. \Compass\ counters this
with the \emph{context pack}: a deduplicated set of evidence snippets and
memory facts, each annotated with an evidence identifier. The answerer is
constrained to the pack; identifiers are surfaced as citations in the answer
and logged for audit. The pack is thus both the grounding mechanism and the
audit trail: what the model saw is exactly what a reviewer can inspect.

\subsection{Targeted symbolic validation over a DPP ontology}
Some DPP questions cannot be answered by finding the right sentence in a
document, because the answer is not written anywhere. Consider ``which
compliance standards does this product require?'' The answer depends on
which components the product contains and on which standard governs each
component type. It must be \emph{derived} by checking structured conditions
against structured facts, and a wrong derivation here is a compliance error,
not a stylistic one. The symbolic layer exists for exactly this class of
questions.

\emph{From documents to a fact graph.} The input to the layer is the set of
product facts that were extracted from the documentation and passed the
validation step of Section~4.2, for example ``product $P$ contains cell
$C$'' or ``the safety test report for $C$ is dated 2024-06-12.'' Each such
fact is stored as an RDF triple, a subject--predicate--object statement such
as $(P, \texttt{hasComponent}, C)$. The collection of all triples known
about a product is its \emph{fact graph}: a machine readable summary of what
the documentation asserts, on which conditions can be checked mechanically.

\emph{The ontology.} A bag of triples is only useful if all parties agree on
what its terms mean, and that shared vocabulary is what the ontology
provides. It declares which classes of entities exist and which relations
may connect them. Our DPP ontology has five core classes, matching what a
product passport must describe: the \textsf{Product} itself, its
\textsf{Component}s, the \textsf{Material}s they use, the
\textsf{ProcessStep}s of their life cycle, and the \textsf{Standard}s they
must satisfy. Five object properties connect these classes
(\texttt{hasComponent}, \texttt{usesMaterial}, \texttt{requiresCompliance},
\texttt{requiresStep}, \texttt{conformsTo}). Domain modules for battery,
printing hardware, and heating systems (plus textiles and food for the
generalization study) extend the core with domain specific component classes
and standards. The design is informed by the concepts and competency
questions of the CIRPASS-2 Ontology Requirements Specification for an EU DPP
core ontology \cite{cirpass2}, and each core class carries a
\texttt{skos:closeMatch} or \texttt{skos:exactMatch} link to the
corresponding proposed CIRPASS-2 concept. These SKOS links are a descriptive
crosswalk for interoperability and audit; they are not used for logical
inference, which would require verified \texttt{owl:equivalentClass} axioms.

\emph{Deriving obligations.} Given the fact graph and the vocabulary, the
layer derives what follows from them. Obligation rules encode domain
knowledge of the form ``components of this type are governed by this
standard'':
\[
\texttt{hasComponent}(p,c) \wedge \texttt{type}(c,t) \wedge
\texttt{governedBy}(t,s) \Rightarrow \texttt{requiresCompliance}(c,s),
\]
read as: if product $p$ contains component $c$, $c$ is of type $t$, and type
$t$ is governed by standard $s$, then $c$ requires compliance with $s$. The
\texttt{governedBy} relation is asserted in the ontology, so every variable
in a rule head is bound in its body. To guarantee that this derivation
always terminates, expressiveness is deliberately restricted to a decidable
fragment (class and property assertions, subclass and subproperty axioms,
domain and range restrictions) evaluated under OWL~2~RL semantics
\cite{motik2009owl2profiles}; entailments are computed by forward chaining
until no rule adds a new triple, and no description logic classifier is
invoked at query time.

\emph{Checking completeness.} Deriving new facts is not the same as noticing
that a required fact is absent, and the second check needs different
machinery. OWL adopts an open world reading in which an absent triple means
``unknown,'' not ``false,'' so entailment alone can never conclude that a
field is missing. Completeness checks are therefore implemented as
explicitly defined validation rules evaluated under a closed world
assumption over the finite fact graph: a required field that is absent is
flagged as missing, and analogous rules flag stale test reports and dates
outside permitted windows. Validation rules act only as conservative vetoes;
they never assert new facts.

\emph{Auditable traces.} Whenever rules contribute to an answer, the system
returns the rule that fired and the triples it matched. An auditor can
therefore replay the derivation and distinguish rule derived conclusions
from purely neural generations. The layer is deliberately \emph{targeted}:
it fires only where structured facts and a matching rule exist, and stays
silent elsewhere. On the reliability suite, rules fire on 7.96\% of queries
with an observed conditional precision of 1.000 (Wilson 95\% interval
$[0.986, 1.000]$; Section~\ref{sec:reliability}).

\subsection{Confidence, calibration, and operating modes}
The final stage turns everything the system knows about a candidate answer
into the answer or abstain decision, which requires a scalar confidence
$c(x)$; the LLM's own generation probability is known to be unreliable as a
probability \cite{guo2017calibration}. \Compass\ instead aggregates
signals that each measure a different way an answer can be weak: retrieval
score margins (was the evidence clearly better than the alternatives?),
agreement across evidence snippets, whether symbolic rules fired, and
generation probabilities when available. The resulting raw score
$\hat{c}(x)$ orders answers but is not yet a probability, so a monotone
calibrator $g$ is fitted on development data such that among questions with
calibrated confidence $c(x)=g(\hat{c}(x))$, approximately a fraction $c(x)$
are correct; isotonic regression is a standard choice
\cite{zadrozny2002transforming,guo2017calibration}. The threshold $\tau$ is
selected on a held out split to meet the coverage a deployment policy
requires and applied unchanged to test data. Each signal is interpretable,
so an abstention can be explained by what was weak: thin retrieval,
disagreeing evidence, or an unverifiable structured condition.

The evaluation harness exports ablation \emph{modes}: \RAGBase\ (retrieval
only), \MemMode\ (adds memory), \SymOnly\ (symbolic only), \MemSymMode\
(memory and symbolic), \RouterMode\ (supervised router), and \Compass\
(full). A reinforcement learned router (\RLMode) is reported only for
completeness; its paired tests show no significant improvement and it is not
claimed as a contribution.

\section{Experimental setting}
\subsection{Studies and data}
We report three complementary studies. (1) A \emph{reliability suite} of
3,429 DPP aligned questions over three corpora (a battery corpus and two
partner documentation corpora), categorised as \textbf{logic} (compliance
questions governed by rules), \textbf{open} (extraction from retrieved
documents), and \textbf{recall} (persistence over long horizons); counts
appear in Table~\ref{tab:counts}. The pooled 3,429-question run supports the
internal mode ablations and selective risk analysis; a scaled release of
6,270 questions (logic 2,649, open 871, recall 2,750) supports the external
baseline comparison. This \emph{verified release} is a deterministically
filtered subset satisfying predefined support and label consistency
criteria: model independent filters exclude items with malformed or
ambiguous labels, gold answers not uniquely supported by the source, or
conflicting knowledge base relations, and the filter definitions and
exclusion lists ship with the release.
(2) A \emph{compositional architecture benchmark} of 3,000 questions built
for this study, whose subtypes require routing across document evidence,
persistent memory, and structured system state (e.g.,
\texttt{memory+document}, \texttt{memory+document+symbolic}); it targets
exactly the multi-source cases that single mechanism systems handle poorly.
(3) A \emph{generalization study} on Open Food Facts (OFF), a public database
of 4.5M food products with 210 fields, from which we generated context bound
evaluation queries spanning nutrition, attribute, missingness,
sustainability, and fairness types. Every question carries a gold label
produced by the dataset generators, and correctness is scored by
deterministic, type specific validators: an open answer must contain the
normalized gold evidence span, a recall answer must match the gold value or a
curated alias after canonicalisation of case, punctuation, and whitespace,
and a logic answer must match the gold label. No LLM judge is involved, so
all scores are exactly reproducible from the released predictions.
Abstentions are scored under the metric definitions of Section~3.

\subsection{Baselines}
In the pooled reliability suite we compare against \RAGBase\ (a strong hybrid
retrieval baseline) and the ablation modes above. On the verified release and
in the compositional benchmark we additionally compare against strong
external baselines: \LongCtx, a GPT-4o-mini baseline supplied with up to
12,000 characters of deterministically retrieval-selected evidence
\cite{liu2024lostinthemiddle}; \LogicLM, a prompted approximation of the
classify-and-reason procedure of Logic-LM \cite{pan2023logiclm}; and, on the
verified release, a prompted LINC-style premise/goal/reasoning baseline
\cite{olausson2023linc}. The latter two do not execute the published systems'
solver pipelines and are named accordingly. All systems use identical row
keys and deterministic validators. The release exports prompts, decoding
parameters, evidence budgets, and known configuration gaps; the archived runs
record the mutable \texttt{gpt-4o-mini} alias but not its exact snapshot, which
the frozen rerun will correct. All quantitative results are produced by an
automated pipeline and exported directly into paper ready tables and figures
to reduce transcription error and support reproducibility.

\subsection{Reliability suite: where the gains come from}

appears verbatim in the retrieved evidence; we read exact 1.000 values as
benchmark saturation, not as a claim that open book QA is solved. The

\section{Discussion and limitations}
\label{sec:disc}
\paragraph{What drives reliability.}
The discriminative signals are logic accuracy, the compositional benchmark,
and the selective risk curves, not pooled open accuracy, which saturates
because retrieved evidence often contains the answer verbatim. Reliability
comes from the \emph{combination} of targeted, high precision symbolic
control (perfect observed precision on the 7.96\% rule fired subset; large
gains on logic and multi-source composition) and selective, evidence
grounded abstention (low AURC, with confidence well calibrated on the unseen
schema but not yet in domain). Neither mechanism alone explains the gains.

\paragraph{Calibration.}
In domain ECE remains high even for the strongest accuracy configuration:
with 10 bin ECE, \Compass\ scores 0.525 and \RAGBase\ 0.487 on the
reliability suite, motivating conservative operating points and further
uncertainty estimation.
The Open Food Facts study shows the calibration pipeline performs well (ECE
0.021) when confidence signals align with evidence availability, so we treat
calibration as a measured reliability axis that is domain sensitive rather
than a uniformly solved property.

\paragraph{In-use evidence.}
\Compass\ is exposed to its target users through the CE-RISE DPP workbench:
practitioners query real partner documentation and see, for every answer, the
retrieved memory and passages, the fired ontology rules and derived triples,
the calibrated confidence, and the abstain status. The audit panel is the
architecture made visible: the same provenance identifiers and rule traces
that the harness scores are what a compliance practitioner inspects. The
workbench has been demonstrated live to consortium partners outside the
development team; production deployment, with logged abstentions and
overrides, is the next step.

\paragraph{Scope.}
The reliability suite covers three DPP corpora; the verified release scales
the logic subset from 295 to 2,649 questions, and the compositional and OFF
studies extend the evaluation to multi-source reasoning and an unseen
schema. The gap between \Compass\ and \SymOnly\ on the pooled suite's
logic questions (0.7085 vs.\ 0.9051) reflects imperfect routing on that
snapshot; the verified release, evaluated after a routing fix, closes it.
Generalisation to further product categories and shifting regulatory
constraints remains future work, and compliance critical decisions should
retain human oversight.

\section{Reproducibility and ethics}
\paragraph{Reproducibility.}
Evaluation outputs are generated by an automated harness and exported
directly into the tables and figures above. We release a public repository
containing the DPP ontology with its CIRPASS-2 crosswalk and per-domain
modules, the
rule set, the evaluation harness, the dataset construction scripts for the
compositional benchmark, and version identifiers and checksums for data and
model checkpoints.

The partner

\paragraph{Ethics and responsible use.}
This work targets factual, document grounded product information and
supports abstention when evidence is missing. The system can still fail
under distribution shift (new categories, altered documentation practices,
changed regulations) and should be used with human oversight for compliance
critical decisions; deployments should expose provenance by default and log
abstentions and overrides for audit.

\section{Conclusion}
We presented \Compass, an architecture for reliable and auditable querying
of Digital Product Passports that composes hybrid retrieval, persistent
memory, and a targeted symbolic layer backed by a DPP ontology informed by
the CIRPASS-2 requirements specification, behind an explicit answer or
abstain interface. Across three studies, \Compass\ substantially improves
selective risk and rule governed accuracy over a strong retrieval baseline,
reaches the ceiling of the verified release and the compositional benchmark
where strong external baselines leave 127 to 321 errors, and transfers to
the unseen Open Food Facts schema with well calibrated confidence (ECE
0.021). The
results show that pairing targeted symbolic control with calibrated
abstention yields trustworthy, auditable querying of structured product
documentation, while in domain probability calibration remains a measurable
open challenge.
