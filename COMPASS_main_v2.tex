\begin{abstract}
Digital Product Passports (DPPs) combine technical, compliance, and
sustainability records. These records are often incomplete and split between
files and structured data. We present \Compass, a question-answering system
that links answers to evidence and can abstain. It combines word- and
meaning-based retrieval, session-scoped text memory, targeted rules, and a
confidence score. On a verified subset of a template-generated, DPP-like
benchmark, \Compass\ answers 6,269 of 6,270 items correctly (0.9998). Three
external baselines share one dated model version
and score 0.9512--0.9702. On a controlled benchmark of 3,000 composition
questions, \Compass\ scores 1.0000. The baselines score 0.8907--0.9593.
Both benchmarks repeat question templates. The comparisons therefore describe
only the observed items. Archived outputs mark 7.96\% of items as symbolic-path
cases. All answers in that subset are labeled correct, but the traces do not
identify the rules used. A separate test uses 4,021 Open Food Facts queries. It gives
the model the relevant row and field names. A rule applied after generation
treats explicit missing-evidence responses as abstentions. Decision accuracy
is 0.972 at 0.730 answer coverage. Fixed-width-bin expected calibration error
is 0.021. The test does not evaluate retrieval. These are controlled tests.
They do not establish performance in real deployments. Confidence also
remains poorly calibrated on the archived DPP run.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Digital Product Passports (DPPs) are meant to make product information easy to
inspect throughout a product's life cycle \cite{eu2024espr,ec_espr_dpp}.
Users may need a technical value, a sustainability record, or evidence of
compliance. A wrong answer can affect an audit or a purchase. It can also
distort a sustainability report. The answer must therefore be supported by
the product record. Plausible wording is not enough.

We developed \Compass\ within CE-RISE, an EU Horizon Europe project on
circular-resource information systems. Our evaluation uses released, curated
DPP-like seed records for batteries, printing hardware, and heating systems.
We demonstrated the project workbench to consortium practitioners
outside the development team. We did not log those sessions in enough detail
for a quantitative usage study.

Several practical problems arise in DPP question answering. Evidence can be
spread across documents or earlier parts of a session. Some answers must be
derived from a rule. Others should be withheld because the required evidence
is absent or conflicting. A long prompt does not remove these problems. A
model can miss evidence because of its position in the prompt
\cite{liu2024lostinthemiddle}. A fluent explanation may also misstate how the
model reached its answer
\cite{turpin2023unfaithfulcot,lanham2023faithfulnesscot}.

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) gives a large language model (LLM)
selected passages before it answers. RAG can improve factual grounding. It
does not by itself apply a compliance rule or show when an answer should be
withheld. \Compass\ adds session-scoped text memory and a small rule layer.
The rule layer acts only when structured facts and a matching rule are
available. Each response carries evidence and a confidence score. We study
the trade-off between answer coverage and error, and we measure calibration
separately
\cite{geifman2019selectivenet,kamath2020selectiveqa,guo2017calibration,traub2024aurc}.

\noindent Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=2pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item We introduce \Compass. It combines word- and meaning-based retrieval,
  session-scoped text memory, and targeted rules. Its interface returns an
  answer with evidence or an abstention.
  \item We use paired tests, risk--coverage curves, calibration, and
  symbolic-path coverage to assess distinct parts of reliability
  \cite{wilson1927probable,mcnemar1947note}.
  \item We release two frozen, template-generated benchmarks with predictions
  for every item: 6,270 verified DPP-like questions and 3,000 controlled
  composition questions. We also
  report a field-aware abstention test with 4,021 Open Food Facts queries.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related work}

\paragraph{Retrieval and memory.}
RAG conditions an answer on retrieved passages. Fusion readers combine
evidence from several passages \cite{lewis2020rag,izacard2021fid}. More context
does not guarantee that the model will use the relevant evidence
\cite{liu2024lostinthemiddle}. Prior systems use learned memory, retrieve from
external stores, or carry state across text segments
\cite{graves2014ntm,khandelwal2020knnlm,bulatov2022rmt}. Our aim is narrower.
\Compass\ retrieves timestamped text within a session and places it beside
document passages. A production system must prevent memory from mixing
products. It must also record each entry's source and preserve a history of
corrections.

\paragraph{Rules and abstention.}
Neurosymbolic methods combine learned components with explicit rules or
solvers \cite{riegel2020lnn,dong2019nlm,manhaeve2018deepproblog}. An ontology
defines the entities and relations in a domain. OWL is the Web Ontology
Language. Its restricted profiles allow efficient inference over an ontology
\cite{motik2009owl2profiles}. LINC and Logic-LM couple an LLM to a symbolic
solver \cite{olausson2023linc,pan2023logiclm}. Our LINC-style and
Logic-LM-style comparisons approximate their prompting procedures. They do
not execute the full published solver systems. Selective prediction asks when a
system should abstain. Risk--coverage analysis measures the resulting
trade-off \cite{geifman2019selectivenet,kamath2020selectiveqa,traub2024aurc}.
Calibration must be tested independently of accuracy
\cite{guo2017calibration,desai2020calibration}.

\paragraph{Positioning.}
\Compass\ does not replace documents with a knowledge graph. Retrieval and
session memory handle most questions. Rules apply only to supported structured
conditions. The released traces preserve evidence identifiers, derived
triples, confidence, and answer paths for inspection. Our evaluation measures
accuracy, abstention, confidence, and symbolic-path use.

\section{Problem formulation and metrics}
For a product query $x$, the system returns an answer $y(x)$ with evidence. It
may instead return the abstention symbol $\bot$. Each output has a confidence
score $c(x)\in[0,1]$. In the selective analysis, a threshold $\tau$ marks
scores below $\tau$ as abstentions. We vary this threshold after all answers
are produced.

\emph{Coverage} is the share of queries answered. \emph{Risk} is the error
rate among those answers. A risk--coverage curve shows how risk changes as the
threshold moves. The area under this curve (AURC) summarizes the trade-off.
Lower is better
\cite{kamath2020selectiveqa,traub2024aurc}. For datasets with deliberately
unanswerable questions, \emph{decision accuracy} credits either a correct
answer or a correct abstention. \emph{Answer accuracy} considers only the
answered queries. We also report abstention precision and recall against the
known answerability labels.

Expected calibration error (ECE) groups predictions by confidence. It is the
weighted mean absolute gap between confidence and accuracy in those groups
\cite{guo2017calibration,desai2020calibration}. We treat it as a
separate metric because an accurate system can still be overconfident.
The Brier score is the mean squared gap between confidence and correctness.
Lower Brier scores are better.
Accuracy estimates include Wilson 95\% intervals
\cite{wilson1927probable}. Exact McNemar tests compare systems on the same
queries \cite{mcnemar1947note}. \emph{Symbolic-path coverage} is the share of
archived rows marked as using a symbolic step. \emph{Symbolic-path accuracy}
is accuracy on that subset. These measures do not identify the exact rule that
executed.

\section{The COMPASS architecture}

\subsection{Design principles and inference path}
The answerer receives explicit evidence and is prompted to cite it. This
instruction cannot ensure that the model ignores prior knowledge. Rules run
only when structured facts support them. Each answer has a confidence score.
We vary its use threshold after all answers are produced.

Figure~\ref{fig:architecture} shows the two answer paths. Retrieval, session
memory, and rules provide candidate evidence. The verified path returns a
field or derived fact directly when possible. It uses the LLM otherwise. The
composition path calls the LLM first, then checks its output against the
evidence. Both paths save selected evidence and raw confidence.

\subsection{Evidence acquisition: hybrid retrieval and session memory}
The retriever averages standardized word-based and dense E5 scores with equal
weight \cite{lewis2020rag,izacard2021fid}. The word-based score uses term
frequency--inverse document frequency (TF--IDF). It rewards words that are
important in a passage but uncommon in the corpus. E5 embeddings compare
meaning rather than exact wording. The system uses TF--IDF alone when dense
search is unavailable. It does not rerank the results.

Session memory stores timestamped text. It retrieves entries from the same
session by cosine similarity, a measure of closeness between embeddings. The
answerer is prompted to cite passage identifiers. If an LLM answer lacks a
bracketed citation, the wrapper appends the first passage identifier. This
enforces a citation format. It does not verify support for each claim. The
saved trace keeps selected identifiers, scores, short snippets, and the answer
path. It does not keep an immutable copy of the full prompt.

The current memory can mix entries from different products in one session. It
does not verify the source of an entry or preserve an append-only correction
history. A production deployment needs all three controls.

\subsection{Targeted symbolic reasoning over a DPP ontology}
Some questions require a derived answer. A product may require a standard
because it contains a certain component. The symbolic layer handles this
limited class of questions.

\emph{Fact graph.} The service loads structured facts from released RDF
files. It does not extract them from session memory at query time. Each fact
is a Resource Description Framework (RDF) triple. A triple has a subject, a
predicate, and an object. For example, $(P,\texttt{hasComponent},C)$ states
that product $P$ contains component $C$.

\emph{Ontology.} The core vocabulary covers products, components, materials,
process steps, and standards. Domain files provide battery, printing,
heating, and textile terms. The vocabulary is informed by the
CIRPASS-2 requirements specification \cite{cirpass2}. Its mapping links are
descriptive. They do not assert logical equivalence.

\emph{Rules.} The implementation uses a finite set of domain-specific SPARQL
\texttt{CONSTRUCT} rules. SPARQL is a query language for RDF data. One battery
rule is:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\texttt{hasComponent}(p,c) \wedge
\texttt{rdf:type}(c,\texttt{Battery})\\
{}\Rightarrow
\texttt{requiresCompliance}(p,\texttt{BatterySafetyStandard}).
\end{aligned}
\]
The conclusion applies to product $p$. At startup, the service first expands
the static graph under the OWL~2~RL rule profile
\cite{motik2009owl2profiles}. It then applies the SPARQL rule list once. It
does not repeat the rules until no new facts appear.

\emph{Missing facts.} OWL uses an open-world assumption. An absent triple is
therefore unknown rather than false. Some released rules use
\texttt{FILTER NOT EXISTS}. This is a local check for a required process step
that is not recorded in the loaded graph. The reasoner does not provide
general checks for missing fields, stale reports, or date windows.

\emph{Recorded evidence.} A symbolic response records derived standards and
process steps. It also records whether the symbolic path was used. It does
not retain every matched premise or the exact SPARQL rule identifier. The
record supports inspection, but not reconstruction of the full proof. Historical
symbolic-path coverage and accuracy appear in Section~\ref{sec:historical}.

\subsection{Confidence, calibration, and operating modes}
The runtime attaches a hand-designed confidence score to each answer. A memory
path uses its top similarity score, clipped to $[0,1]$. A retrieval path maps
its top search score to the $0$--$1$ range with an S-shaped function. A
symbolic path uses an explicit verdict score when one is available. Otherwise,
it uses 0.5. When several paths contribute, the implementation keeps the
largest score. It does not use snippet agreement or token-generation
probabilities.

On every pooled row, \texttt{confidence\_cal} equals the raw score. No fitted
calibration map is recoverable. We therefore evaluate the stored scores as
they are. After the answers are produced, we vary the threshold $\tau$ and
plot risk against coverage. The online endpoint has no calibration model or
shared threshold. The Open Food Facts study also uses the model's reported
confidence without fitting a later calibration model.

The pooled study also reports configurations that isolate parts of the system.
\RAGBase\ uses retrieval only. \MemMode\ uses memory only. \SymOnly\ uses
rules only. \MemSymMode\ combines memory and rules. \RouterMode\ uses the
learned router. \RLMode\ uses the project's experimental reinforcement-learning
(RL) policy. The verified release records the project system as
\texttt{ADAPTIVERAG}. The composition study
records its full evidence-combining mode as \texttt{AUTO\_COMPOSE}. Both modes
appear as \Compass\ in the paper, while the release keeps their internal
names.

\section{Experimental setting}
\subsection{Studies and data}
The main DPP study uses 6,270 items from a frozen, template-generated DPP-like
benchmark. It contains 2,649 logic questions, 871 open-extraction questions,
and 2,750 recall questions. The generator uses the released ontology and
curated seed records for batteries, printing hardware, and heating systems.
This is not a natural-query or regulatory DPP corpus. Fixed filters remove
malformed labels, ambiguous or unsupported reference answers, and conflicting
graph relations. The filters do not inspect system predictions.

The second study is a controlled, generated benchmark with 3,000 questions.
Its six subtypes require document evidence, session memory, structured facts,
or combinations of these sources. The third study uses 4,021 Open Food Facts
queries \cite{openfoodfacts}. It covers nutrition, product attributes, missing
fields, sustainability, and fairness. The evaluator receives the relevant product row
or aggregate missingness row. It also receives the names of the fields needed
by the query.

We also report an archived, template-generated DPP-like run with 3,429
questions. It predates the frozen release. Of these, 295 are logic questions,
2,297 are open questions, and 837 are recall questions. We use this run only
for internal component comparisons, symbolic-path coverage, and selective
analysis. We could not rerun it because 2,084 rows lack recoverable reference
labels.

The verified release uses deterministic scoring for each question type. A
logic answer must agree with the expected yes/no label. For open and recall
questions, the scorer normalizes the text and checks for the expected answer.
The composition study requires at least one accepted value from
every expected source group. Open Food Facts uses missing-field labels and
normalized text matching. Numeric answers allow the larger of 0.01 or 0.5\%
error. Percentages allow 0.25 percentage points. No study uses an LLM judge.

Both frozen benchmarks repeat question forms and source entities. After
replacing product identifiers and numbers, the verified set has 289 distinct
question forms and the composition set has 497. Wilson intervals and row-level
McNemar tests therefore describe the observed benchmark items. They do not
justify claims about a wider population of independent, naturally occurring
queries.

\subsection{Baselines}
The pooled suite uses \RAGBase\ and the internal component comparisons. The
frozen studies add three GPT-4o-mini baselines. \LongCtx\ is a retrieval
baseline. It receives
up to 12,000 selected characters \cite{liu2024lostinthemiddle}. A
Logic-LM-style prompt asks for classification and reasoning
\cite{pan2023logiclm}. A LINC-style prompt supplies a premise, goal, and
reasoning instruction \cite{olausson2023linc}. The last two are approximations.
They do not run the full published solver systems.

The three external baselines and all model calls in the composition study
resolve to \texttt{gpt-4o-mini-2024-07-18}. The systems use the same questions
and scoring rules within each study. The release includes prompts, decoding
settings, evidence budgets, predictions, and known departures from the
published baseline procedures. The older Open Food Facts run retains only the
\texttt{gpt-4o-mini} alias. Its dated model version was not saved.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}
\subsection{Frozen verified release}
\label{sec:verified}
The archived pooled suite contains 295 logic questions. The frozen verified
release contains 2,649 logic questions among 6,270 questions in total.
\Compass\ answers 6,269 questions correctly. Its accuracy is 0.9998, with a
Wilson 95\% interval of [0.9991, 1.0000]. \LogicLM\ scores 0.9702,
\LongCtx\ 0.9662, and the LINC-style baseline 0.9512
(Table~\ref{tab:verified}).

The item-level paired tests use the same 6,270 rows for every system. The
numbers of \Compass-only/baseline-only wins are 212/1 against \LongCtx, 306/1
against LINC-style, and 187/1 against \LogicLM. Every exact McNemar test has
$p<10^{-10}$. These results show near-perfect item accuracy on this controlled
subset. They do not show that DPP question answering is solved.

This is a system-level comparison. \Compass\ uses a deterministic rule or
field-extraction path on 6,266 rows. It uses an LLM on four rows. Those four
records retain the \texttt{gpt-4o-mini} alias, but not its dated model version
or temperature. Each external baseline uses the dated model version on every
row. This missing version information limits reproducibility.

\subsection{Controlled compositional benchmark}
\label{sec:composition}
The release names the full \Compass\ configuration \texttt{AUTO\_COMPOSE}.
It answers all 3,000 benchmark questions correctly. \LongCtx\ answers 2,878
(0.9593), \LogicLM\ 2,822 (0.9407), and LINC-style 2,672 (0.8907).

\texttt{AUTO\_COMPOSE} attempted an LLM call on every row. Deterministic output
checks then changed or supplemented 198 answers. In 20 final traces, the LLM
answer was discarded. The 1.0000 score belongs to this full system, not to
the LLM alone.

The largest gap occurs on the 225 questions that require memory, document,
and symbolic evidence. Accuracies on this subtype are 1.000 for \Compass,
0.707 for \LongCtx, 0.631 for \LogicLM, and 0.524 for LINC-style. The
document--memory subtype also separates the systems. By contrast, all
baselines are near perfect on several memory--symbolic subtypes. Every
item-level paired test against \Compass\ has $p<10^{-36}$.

This benchmark is generated and controlled. Each system receives the evidence
specified for its condition. It measures performance on designed composition
tasks, not on naturally occurring queries.

\subsection{Field-aware abstention on Open Food Facts}
\label{sec:off}
We evaluate a GPT-4o-mini answerer given row context from Open Food Facts. The
data uses a different schema. The evaluator receives the relevant product row or
aggregate missingness row. It also receives the names of the fields required
by the query. This is a field-aware row-context test. It does not evaluate
retrieval over the raw data dump or transfer of the symbolic reasoner. A direct
lookup is labeled unanswerable when its required field is absent. A missingness
question asks whether a field is present, so it remains answerable.

On 4,021 queries, decision accuracy is 0.972. Answer accuracy is 0.963 at
0.730 coverage (Table~\ref{tab:off}). Fixed-width-bin ECE is 0.021, and AURC
is 0.025. These figures use a text-based rule applied after generation during
rescoring. It treats answers such as ``Missing'' or ``Insufficient context'' as
abstentions, even when the model's JSON flag says otherwise. Missingness
questions are exempt because ``missing'' is a valid factual answer. The raw
flag marks 385 abstentions. Its recall against the unanswerable labels is 0.353.
The text-based rule reclassifies 702 more outputs, producing 1,087 abstentions.
Of these, 1,085 match the unanswerable labels. Effective abstention precision
is 0.998 and recall is 1.000.
Sustainability is the weakest category. Its answer accuracy is 0.787 at 0.380
coverage. The fairness subset has only 19 questions. It is too small to
support a separate claim.

\section{Discussion and limitations}
\label{sec:disc}
\paragraph{Interpretation.}
The frozen verified and compositional studies provide the main evidence. The
verified release contains far more logic questions than the archived run. The
controlled benchmark shows the largest losses when a baseline must combine
document and memory evidence. Open and recall questions in the archived run
are saturated. They offer little separation between architectures. The
archived comparison marks 273 cases as using a symbolic path. All are labeled
correct, but this result does not isolate rule effects. Thresholding also
reduces observed risk. Missing reference labels make this evidence secondary.

\paragraph{Calibration.}
High accuracy does not imply reliable probabilities. The archived field named
\texttt{confidence\_cal} is identical to the raw score and should not be read as
a calibrated probability. Equal-count bins contain
similar numbers of predictions. Fixed-width bins divide the confidence range
into equal intervals. With equal-count bins, ECE is 0.5247 for \Compass\ on the
archived run. Its bootstrap 95\% interval
is [0.5181, 0.5311], and its Brier score is 0.3165. \RAGBase\ has ECE 0.4873,
with interval [0.4771, 0.4967]. Both are poorly calibrated in the source
domains. Open Food Facts gives better values. Its equal-count ECE is 0.0328,
with interval [0.0276, 0.0377], and its Brier score is 0.0287. Its
fixed-width-bin ECE is 0.021. The studies differ in task and domain. These
results do not show that one calibration method will transfer.

\paragraph{Filtering and validator checks.}
\label{sec:filtering}
The verified filter removes 645 of 6,915 rows. It checks source support,
ambiguity, and whether each reference answer is a single value. It does not
read system predictions. Before filtering, accuracies are 0.9721 for \Compass,
0.9424 for \LongCtx, 0.9202 for
LINC-style, and 0.9503 for \LogicLM. Every score rises after filtering. The
gain is therefore not unique to \Compass.

The current scorer agrees with all archived labels in a sample of 100
errors split almost equally across the three external baselines. Manual review
flags 12 likely false negatives. In those cases, the exact-span rule requires
an extra qualifier. This small audit does not establish the scorer's overall
error rate. Static code
inspection finds no imports between the question generator and runtime rule
modules. They share the released ontology and seed data by design.

\paragraph{In-use evidence.}
The CE-RISE workbench has been demonstrated to consortium practitioners
outside the development team. It shows retrieved passages, session memory,
derived triples, raw confidence, and the selected answer path. We did not log
historical session counts, user counts, or feedback. These demonstrations
exposed the system to outside practitioners. They do not constitute a field-use
or effectiveness study. Production evaluation remains future work.

\paragraph{Scope.}
The verified subset excludes ambiguous and unsupported items. The verified and
compositional benchmarks are template-generated and reuse source entities. The
LINC-style and Logic-LM-style baselines approximate their published procedures.
They do not run the full solver systems. The external baselines and
compositional run use the same
dated model version. Four verified \Compass\ records retain only the
model alias. The Open Food Facts study uses a supplied row and required field
names. It does not test end-to-end retrieval or ontology transfer. The run used
temperature zero. Its outputs retain the \texttt{gpt-4o-mini} alias but not a
dated model version. We have not tested further product domains, model families,
changing regulations, or production memory controls. Compliance-critical
decisions still require human review.

\section{Reproducibility and ethics}
\paragraph{Reproducibility.}
The \href{https://github.com/aeturzo/LLMEnhance}{public repository} includes a
\href{https://github.com/aeturzo/LLMEnhance/tree/ijckg2026-phase-b-frozen-20260719}{frozen release tag}.
It records run commits, file
checksums, prompts, decoding settings, and resolved model identifiers where
available. It also contains the questions, reference labels, and predictions
for every item needed to recompute the verified and compositional results.
Partner documents cannot be redistributed. Four verified \Compass\ LLM rows
lack a dated model identifier. The Open Food Facts outputs also retain only
the model alias. The pooled component comparison remains historical because
2,084 rows lack recoverable reference labels.

\paragraph{Ethics and responsible use.}
Evidence can become stale as products, documents, or regulations change. A
deployment should show its evidence sources and log abstentions and human
overrides. Compliance-critical answers require human review.

\section{Conclusion}
\Compass\ combines hybrid retrieval, session-scoped text memory, and targeted
rules. On the template-generated verified benchmark, it answers 6,269 of 6,270
questions correctly. The external baselines score 0.9512--0.9702. On the
template-generated compositional benchmark, the full system answers all 3,000
questions correctly. The baselines leave 122--328 errors. With later text-based
rescoring, the Open Food Facts test reaches 0.972 decision accuracy.

Archived final answers marked as symbolic had no observed errors. The traces do
not identify the rules used. In 702 Open Food Facts outputs, the text signaled
missing evidence although the JSON abstention flag was false. These studies do
not establish performance on unrestricted natural queries or in production.
The stored confidence scores also remain poorly calibrated on the archived DPP
run.
